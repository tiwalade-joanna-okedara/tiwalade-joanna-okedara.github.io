The edit pass: turning a slop draft about AI pentesting into something a red team lead would finish

A spec piece for my XBOW application, showing how I edit. The "before" is a draft I wrote on purpose in the style that LLMs and rushed marketing teams produce. It is not XBOW's writing. The "after" uses only claims that are public and sourced.

Before

In today's rapidly evolving threat landscape, organizations face unprecedented cybersecurity challenges. Cutting-edge AI-powered penetration testing solutions are revolutionizing the way businesses protect their digital assets. By leveraging advanced machine learning algorithms, these innovative platforms can identify vulnerabilities faster than ever before, empowering security teams to stay ahead of sophisticated threat actors. With AI pentesting, organizations can achieve comprehensive coverage, reduce risk, and unlock unparalleled peace of mind. It's not just a tool; it's a game-changer for the future of security.

It's 80 words. Read it again and try to find one thing a CISO could act on, or one claim a researcher could check. There isn't one.

What I'd flag

  1. Throat-clearing opener. "In today's rapidly evolving threat landscape" tells a security reader nothing they didn't know when they woke up. Cut it.
  2. Adjectives doing the work of evidence. "Cutting-edge", "innovative", "unparalleled", "sophisticated". Every one is a claim with no proof attached. A skeptical reader treats each one as a reason to stop trusting the piece.
  3. "Identify vulnerabilities faster than ever before." Faster than what, measured how? And "identify" is the wrong verb for XBOW's whole argument. Finding a possible vulnerability is the cheap part. Proving it is the point.
  4. "Comprehensive coverage". Of what? Which applications, which classes of bug, how often? Unscoped coverage claims are the kind the product may not keep. Cut, or make specific.
  5. "Peace of mind". No security leader buys peace of mind. They buy evidence they can take to engineering and to the board.
  6. "Not just a tool; it's a game-changer." The single most recognisable AI-writing pattern there is. Delete on sight.

After

In 2024, XBOW matched a principal pentester's 40-hour manual assessment in 28 minutes. Speed is the easy part to sell. The harder part is what arrives at the end.

Most scanners hand you possibilities. XBOW hands you proof: every finding comes with the working exploit, the request, the response and the steps to reproduce it. Engineering can't argue with it, and once a fix ships, XBOW runs the same attack again to confirm the issue is closed.

A finding without a working exploit is a hypothesis. That's the standard we test to.

It's 92 words, about the same length. Every sentence is either a specific, sourced claim or a clear position.

The checklist I ran

  • Does the first sentence say something specific and true?
  • Is every number current, verified and sourced?
  • Is every claim something the product actually does today?
  • Would a practitioner respect it, and could a CISO follow it?
  • Does it sound like one company, not like every company?
  • Is there a single sentence an LLM would have written by default? If so, rewrite it.
